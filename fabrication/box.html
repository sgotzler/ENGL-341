Game Box Dielines

Laser cut files and print templates for your prototype box

Set the finished size of your box and the board you're cutting. The page draws a laser-ready base and telescoping lid, checks the fit on your sheet and the BeAM beds, and draws the print templates for the wrap. Making a board too? Use the Game Board Dielines kit.

Finished box (inches)
Material

Send this file to the laser as you saved it. The Universal lasers read only pure red (#FF0000) as a cut and pure blue (#0000FF) as a score, at 0.001″. Recoloring, moving or rescaling a line, or switching an Illustrator file to CMYK, can make the laser skip it. A 0.001″ line looks faint or invisible in a browser; that's correct. To check it, zoom in to 400% or more, or use View › Outline in Illustrator.

Dimensions

Will it fit?

Bed sizes from BeAM: Murray has an 18 × 24 Universal and two 18 × 32 Trotecs; Hanes has an 18 × 24 and a 24 × 48 Universal; Carmichael has an 18 × 24.

Assembly

Spraying isn't allowed at BeAM. Do every spray-adhesive and spray-finish step outdoors or in a well-ventilated room, over scrap cardboard.

  1. Cut a scrap test first. Tune the blue score so it cuts about halfway through the board without breaking out.
  2. Fold each wall away from the scored face, so the score opens on the outside, where the wrap hides it.
  3. Two facing walls run the full edge. The other two are trimmed by twice the board thickness, so they fit between the full walls at the corners instead of overlapping them. Join each inside corner with a strip of wetted gummed tape.
  4. Print the wrap at 100% scale, extending art to the cyan bleed. Glue with PVA or spray mount, center the tray on the bottom panel, and fold the turn-in over the rim.
  5. Test the lid on the wrapped base before gluing the lid's wrap. Adjust clearance here if it binds.

Box art, start to finish

A full-depth lid covers the base completely when the box is closed. Put your effort into the lid wrap: its top panel is the cover and its four walls are what people see on the shelf. The base wrap can be a solid color or simple pattern.

1Save the template

Set your box size above, open the Wrap · lid tab, and save the SVG. Do the same for Wrap · base. Each file is drawn at real size, bleed included, so you never set a page size yourself.

2Open it in Illustrator

UNC students get Adobe Creative Cloud free through Software Distribution. In Illustrator, choose File › Open and pick the SVG. Open Window › Layers: you'll see three groups.

  • GUIDES_delete-before-printing · the cyan bleed band and the panel labels
  • PRINT_fold-lines · light gray dashes
  • PRINT_trim-line · the outline you'll cut on

Click the lock icon next to the layer that holds them.

3Make an art layer underneath

Choose Layer › New Layer, name it Art, and drag it below the template layer. Do all your work on Art. Paint every panel right out to the outer edge of the cyan band, including the turn-in strips, which show inside the rim.

Bring in drawings or photos with File › Place. Make them at 300 ppi at the size they'll print, or they'll look soft.

4Keep type off the folds

Keep titles, credits, and anything important at least ¼″ inside a fold or trim line. Folds wander a little when you glue, and type that crosses one will look shoddy.

The lid's top panel reads right side up with the front wall at the top of the file.

5Clean up and export

Unlock the template, select the GUIDES_delete-before-printing group, and delete it. Leave the fold and trim lines; they're thin and light enough to disappear at the edges.

Choose File › Save a Copy, pick Adobe PDF, and use the High Quality Print preset. Under Marks and Bleeds, set every bleed to 0, because the file already includes it.

6Check the size before you print

Open the PDF and check the document size against the numbers at the top of this section. If they don't match, something rescaled the file. Go back to step 1 rather than resizing by hand.

7Send your files to Dr. Gotzler

Don't print these yourself. Large-format printing for the class is paid for and sent out together. Email your PDFs to sgotzler@unc.edu, one file per print, named by team and part: teamname_lid.pdf, teamname_base.pdf, teamname_board.pdf.

Every file is printed at 100% / actual size on matte paper, so what you exported in step 5 is exactly what comes back.

8Measure, then trim

When your print comes back, the lid's top panel should measure between its fold lines. If it does, cut along the trim line with a craft knife and steel ruler, and continue with step 4 of Assembly above.

Using Inkscape instead (free)

Open the SVG with File › Open. In Layers and Objects, add a new layer below the template groups and work there. Place images with File › Import. Before exporting, delete the GUIDES_delete-before-printing group. Then choose File › Save a Copy, pick PDF, and keep the page size as it is. Steps 6 to 8 are the same.
